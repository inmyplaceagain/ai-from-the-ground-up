"Artificial Intelligence" means something different every decade. Here's the shortest history you'll ever read — just enough to understand where everything came from and why.

## The moving goalpost

AI has a funny pattern: once a computer can do something, people stop calling it AI. Chess? That was AI in the 1950s. Spam filters? AI in the 2000s. Now they're just... software.

The constant definition: **making computers do things that seem to require intelligence**. What counts as "intelligence" keeps changing.

## The 30-second timeline

**1950s–60s: Logic and search.** Can a computer prove theorems? Play chess? Solve puzzles? The idea: intelligence is reasoning, so write programs that reason. These worked for simple problems but choked on anything messy or ambiguous.

**1970s–80s: Expert systems.** Can we capture what a human expert knows as a set of rules? "If the patient has fever AND cough, consider flu." This worked well in narrow domains but fell apart at scale. You couldn't write enough rules.

**1990s–2000s: Machine learning.** Instead of writing rules by hand, let the computer *learn* them from data. Show it 10,000 emails labeled "spam" or "not spam," and it figures out the patterns. This was the big shift.

**2010s: Deep learning.** Machine learning with neural networks — lots of layers, lots of data, lots of compute. Image recognition, speech-to-text, translation. Suddenly computers could do things that felt genuinely intelligent.

**2020s: Generative AI and agents.** Models that don't just classify — they create. They write, draw, code, and reason. And now they use tools, make plans, and work in teams.

## What this means for you

Each era didn't replace the last — it built on it. The expert system idea (rules + knowledge) is alive in every RAG pipeline. Machine learning powers every recommendation engine. Deep learning runs every LLM.

## Two flavors of AI

You'll hear these terms thrown around:

**Narrow AI** (what we have today) — good at one specific task. A model that classifies images can't write poetry. ChatGPT can write poetry but can't drive a car. Every AI system today is narrow.

**General AI** (what researchers are working toward) — a system that can do any intellectual task a human can. We're not there yet. Whether current approaches will get us there is an open debate.

For this course, we're focused on narrow AI — the stuff you can build and ship today.

## Why "from the ground up"?

When you understand each layer, the latest advances stop being magic. GPT isn't magic — it's a transformer (a specific architecture) trained on text (machine learning) using neural networks (layers of simple math) that generates tokens one at a time (a learned process).
