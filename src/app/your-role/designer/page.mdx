## What changed

A year ago, AI design tools were party tricks. Generate a random UI from a prompt, get something that looked impressive in a tweet but fell apart the moment you tried to use it in a real project.

In 2026, the tools finally understand **your** design system.

**Figma Make** generates complete UI layouts using your actual components, your tokens, your spacing scale. Not generic boxes — your buttons, your cards, your type hierarchy. You describe what you need, it builds with the pieces you already defined.

**Figma AI Agent** works directly on your canvas. It can rearrange layouts, apply styles, generate variations — all while respecting the constraints you've set up in your design system.

**Google Stitch** takes text descriptions, rough sketches, or even reference images and turns them into responsive UI with exportable code.

**v0 by Vercel** lets you describe a component and get a working React implementation instantly, then refine it conversationally — "make the header sticky," "add a dark mode toggle."

## Before and after

Here's what a real design workflow looks like now.

**Before:** You get a brief for a settings page. You manually create 5+ layout variations in Figma. You tweak spacing, test different arrangements, adjust for mobile. You export assets. You write specs for the developer. This takes a day, maybe two.

**After:** You describe the settings page to Figma Make. It generates three variations using your design system's components. You pick the one closest to what you imagined, refine it conversationally — "move the profile section above billing," "use the compact card variant." You spend your time on the decisions that matter: the information hierarchy, the flow, the edge cases. Builder.io converts your final design directly into production-ready code.

The day-long task takes an hour. But you didn't skip the thinking — you skipped the pixel-pushing.

## How it actually works (the theory)

Two chapters in this course explain the tech behind your new tools:

**[Diffusion Models](/generative-ai/diffusion-models)** — how image generation works. The "add noise, then learn to remove it" process. This is the engine behind Midjourney, DALL-E, and the visual generation features in Figma and Stitch.

**[CNNs: How Computers See](/deep-learning/cnns)** — how AI understands images. Sliding windows that detect edges, then shapes, then objects. This is why AI can look at your mockup and understand "that's a navigation bar" instead of "that's some rectangles."

You don't need to train models. But understanding how they work makes you better at using them — you'll know why certain prompts work better, and why the AI sometimes gets confused by unusual layouts.

## What stays human

AI is very good at generating options. It's terrible at knowing which option is right.

**Taste.** The AI can generate 50 color palettes. It can't tell you which one makes your fintech app feel trustworthy without feeling boring.

**Brand judgment.** Your design system exists because someone made opinionated decisions about what this product should feel like. AI follows rules. You make the rules.

**User empathy.** The AI doesn't know that your users are stressed when they hit the billing page, or that they're excited on the onboarding flow. You design for emotions, not just layouts.

**Rule-breaking.** The best design moments happen when someone intentionally breaks the system — an oversized heading, an unexpected illustration, a layout that doesn't follow the grid. AI optimizes for the average. You optimize for the moment.

Your tools got dramatically better. Your judgment is what makes them useful.
